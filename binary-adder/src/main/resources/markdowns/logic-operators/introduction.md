# **Low-level logic circuits**

Welcome to the introduction of low-level logic circuits. Before diving into the project, we must first establish a clear understanding of what a low-level logic circuit actually is.

## 1. Explanation of low-level logic circuits

### 1.1 What is an electronic circuit ⚡?

#### The author's definition:
> "A circuit—specifically, an electronic circuit—is a combination of basic electronic elements (such as resistors, transistors, capacitors, and diodes) connected to one another to control and direct the electrical energy passing through them."

#### Wikipedia's definition:
> "An electronic circuit is composed of individual electronic components, such as resistors, transistors, capacitors, inductors and diodes, connected by conductive wires or traces through which electric current can flow. It is a type of electrical circuit. For a circuit to be referred to as electronic, rather than electrical, generally at least one active component must be present."

### 1.2 What does "Low-level" mean 🤖?

In this context, the term **"low-level"** signifies that **no integrated circuits (ICs) are used**. Instead, the system relies entirely on discrete, fundamental components like resistors, transistors, and diodes. 

To better contrast this with modern electronics, consider this definition of an integrated circuit from *Synopsys*:

> "An integrated circuit (IC) — commonly called a chip — is a compact, highly efficient semiconductor device that contains a multitude of interconnected electronic components such as transistors, resistors, and capacitors, all fabricated on a single piece of silicon. This revolutionary technology forms the backbone of modern electronics, enabling high-speed, miniaturized, and reliable devices found in everything from smartphones and computers to medical equipment and vehicles."

### 1.3 Conclusion 🏆

By combining these concepts, we arrive at our definitive definition: 

A **low-level logic circuit** is a discrete electronic circuit built entirely from fundamental components (resistors, transistors, diodes) to implement a fundamental logic operator—such as AND, OR, NOT, NAND, or NOR—without relying on pre-fabricated microchips.
